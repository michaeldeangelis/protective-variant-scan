# Hypothesis

## Question

Among human genes, does carrying a predicted loss-of-function variant go with a better result on a fixed set of brain and body traits, hold up in an independent cohort, and avoid harm on nine health outcomes?

## Why this question

Most cognitive and physical traits are highly polygenic: thousands of common variants, each with a tiny effect, and a share of the population signal that is family environment. Single-gene levers are expected mainly where rare variants sit in core genes. PCSK9 is the worked example, with its diabetes trade-off. The scan looks for other such genes in public data and reports them as candidates, never as proven levers.

## What would count

Defined before the run in [experiments.md](experiments.md): the 13-trait panel and its direction of benefit, the discovery threshold, the replication rule, the trade-off screen, the tiers, the controls, and the verdicts PASS, LEAD and KILL.

## What it cannot show

Causation beyond what the data design allows. Effects of turning a gene up, of drugs, or of editing. Effects in ancestries the tables do not report. Effects of variants too rare to test at gene level. Anything about the common-variant score.
